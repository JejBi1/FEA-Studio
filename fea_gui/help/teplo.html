Heat

The Heat step computes heat conduction in the parts as a steady state or as a time history (transient case). Boundary conditions: prescribed temperature, heat flux, power, convection, radiation to the surroundings, radiation between faces of a cavity and heat generation; interfaces of parts can have a thermal contact conductance. The temperature field can then load the mechanical analysis as thermal stress.

What the step can do

  • A thermal case is a separate heat problem with its own boundary conditions; several cases are computed separately.
  • Steady state: heat input must have a way out – at least one prescribed temperature, convection or radiation in every group of connected parts.
  • Transient case: time history from the initial temperature with a fixed step; the conditions act from time 0 and stay constant. Materials then also need density ρ and c_p.
  • Thermal stress is not computed here: add the load “Temperature (thermal stress)” to a mechanical case in Loads.
  • Heat only is allowed: no supports or mechanical loads needed; empty mechanical load cases are then not sent to the analysis.

Material properties in Materials: λ [W/(m·K)] for conduction, c_p [J/(kg·K)] with ρ for transient cases, α [10⁻⁶/K] for thermal stress. Built-in examples: S235 steel λ = 53.3 W/(m·K), α = 12·10⁻⁶/K, c_p = 440 J/(kg·K); PLA λ = 0.13 W/(m·K), α = 68·10⁻⁶/K, c_p = 1800 J/(kg·K).

Procedure

  1. Create a case with the + button and name it (adding a condition creates it automatically).
  2. Decide whether it is transient; enter duration, step and number of snapshots.
  3. Select faces in 3D, choose the Type and value, press Add from selection. A heat source needs only a part.
  4. Check the Study temperatures (reference and initial).
  5. For thermal stress add “Temperature (thermal stress)” to a mechanical case in Loads with “thermal case: …”.

Contact conductance (all thermal cases)

Without it, touching parts are in perfect thermal contact. An interface with a thermal contact conductance h_c [W/(m²·K)] has a temperature jump ΔT = q / h_c (CalculiX *GAP CONDUCTANCE). Interfaces are model data – they apply to all thermal cases.

A temperature jump needs two sets of nodes on the interface: when the interface is added, one of the parts is meshed separately (the *TIE list of the Joints step, logged) and stays mechanically tied by *TIE – the mechanics is unchanged except for the non-conforming mesh (CalculiX backend). A part with a bolt imprint is not chosen. Deleting the interface does not switch the separate mesh off.

A contact pair can have its own h_c (field Thermal conductance in the Contacts step); the other contact pairs are in perfect thermal contact in the heat analysis. The estimate of h_c is indicative (±50 % and more) – measure it or take a datasheet value when the result depends on it.

Heat balance

The equivalent of force equilibrium for a steady state. The heat of all conditions (faces by exact integration) must sum to zero: the residual must not exceed 0.1 % of the sum of absolute fluxes, otherwise the result is discarded. Results show Boundary condition heat [W] (+ = into the body) and “Heat balance: input … W, output … W, residual … W (… %)”. Transient cases are not checked (heat is stored in the parts).

A cavity appears in the heat table as one row with its net heat (a closed cavity ≈ 0; a larger value is reported as a warning – the faces probably do not enclose the cavity, use an open one). The heat of each cavity face and the heat through each contact conductance interface are listed separately (see Results) – they are internal exchanges and are not part of the balance.

Thermal stress

In Loads choose Temperature (thermal stress) and in Temperature from:

  • thermal case: … – the field of a computed case; for a transient case enter Time or leave end of case checked (linear interpolation between snapshots).
  • uniform part temperature – T [°C] on the parts checked in the Parts list (none checked = all). It overrides the thermal field; at an interface of parts with different temperatures the node gets the average.

A load case takes at most one thermal field. The stress is proportional to ΔT, so it superposes and works in fatigue. The equilibrium check refers to the restrained-expansion force Σ E·α·|ΔT|·V / L. CalculiX and the large-model solver handle it; thermal cases always run in CalculiX.

Caution: stiffness and strength do not change with temperature. For plastics (PLA softens above ~50 °C) and steel above ~300 °C the result is non-conservative. Thermal expansion of 3D printing is isotropic.

Bolts and temperature

  • Bolts do not conduct heat (a beam has no volume); heat flows only through the faces where parts touch.
  • A bolt expands with the average temperature of its bearing faces, α = 11.5·10⁻⁶/K (stainless “A…” 16·10⁻⁶/K). The mismatch changes the bolt force; stiffness and preload do not change.
  • A bolt with a CAD clearance in its stack does not expand thermally (warning).

3D printing and heat

For printed parts the conductivity and heat capacity of each element are multiplied by the solid fraction (shell + infill). A part with 20 % infill conducts worse than a solid one. This is an indicative parallel-conduction model; air in the infill is neglected.

Limitations

  • “Radiation to surroundings” has no mutual irradiation of faces; radiation between faces needs the cavity radiation condition. View factors are computed by CalculiX without shadowing checks by other parts outside the cavity faces.
  • Touching parts share nodes, so contact is perfect unless an interface has a contact conductance (see above). h_c is constant – it does not follow the contact pressure or temperature. Contact pairs with a conforming split interface are in perfect contact in heat (no h_c).
  • λ, c_p, h and ε do not depend on temperature. No fluid flow or phase change; convection is a boundary condition with a given h.
  • Transient conditions are constant in time; each transient case starts from the initial temperature.
  • MYSTRAN (cross-check) does not convert heat.

In the examples

Example 1 “Printed bracket”

No thermal cases; the 30 °C in 3D printing only affects the creep estimate. To try thermal stress, add “Temperature (thermal stress)” to a new load case as a uniform part temperature of 40 °C. The part is held by a cylindrical support and a rest, so you see the restrained PLA expansion (α = 68·10⁻⁶/K).

Example 2 “Assembly”

Reference 20 °C. Both thermal cases share:

  • Power 15 W into the face “Post hole” (heater in the Ø10 hole),
  • convection h = 10 W/(m²·K), T ambient 25 °C on “Arm surface” (all arm faces except the seat on the base),
  • prescribed temperature 20 °C on “Base bottom”.

“Operation – steady state”: in the heat table the hole (+15 W), the arm convection and the floor temperature must balance within 0.1 %; the arm can gain heat by convection where it is colder than the surroundings (25 °C). “Ramp-up – 1 hour”: transient, 3600 s, step 120 s (30 steps), 6 snapshots at 0, 600, 1200 … 3600 s – the steel post heats up. The mechanical case “Heating in operation” takes the steady-state temperature field. Steel (12·10⁻⁶/K) and PLA (68·10⁻⁶/K) expand differently: the arm, bolts and welds are stressed, and the bolts expand with their bearing-face temperature (warning). The arm conducts only by its solid fraction (25 % Gyroid infill); bolts conduct no heat.

Common problems

  • “Thermal case 'X': steady-state case has no path for heat to leave – specify a prescribed temperature, convection, or radiation”
    Add a heat sink, or make the case transient.
  • “Thermal case 'X': parts without a heat sink (no path to a prescribed temperature, convection, or radiation – the steady-state problem has no solution)”
    The listed parts are not connected by a face to the heat exit; a bolt does not conduct heat. Add convection to them, or exclude them in Joints.
  • “Material 'X' (part 'Y') has no thermal conductivity k [W/(m·K)]”, or “has no density and specific heat capacity c_p”, or “has no coefficient of thermal expansion α [1/K] (thermal load)”
    Fill in λ, c_p or α in Materials. This applies to all meshed parts.
  • “Thermal case 'X': heat balance does not close (residual … W, … % of fluxes). The result is invalid.”
    Usually overlapping conditions on the same faces (temperature with flux or convection; two temperatures on a shared edge), or unconverged radiation. Check for overlaps and that the CalculiX iterations finished in the log.
  • “Contact conductance 'X': parts 'A' and 'B' share a mesh (perfect contact) – mesh one of them separately”
    The part was removed from the separately meshed parts; add the interface again (it switches the separate mesh on) or tick the part in the *TIE list of the Joints step.
  • “Contact conductance 'X': parts 'A' and 'B' do not touch (no common faces within … mm)”
    The parts have a gap in CAD – use cavity radiation (or convection) across the gap, or fix the geometry.
  • “Closed cavity 'X': net heat of the faces … W …” (warning)
    The selected faces do not enclose the cavity; uncheck “closed cavity” so the opening sees the ambient temperature.
  • “Heat flux is stored only for the end of a transient case – choose the last time.”
    Choose the last time to display flux, or shorten the duration.
  • “Thermal case 'X': 50000 time steps – the analysis will take long”, “the time step must be 0 (automatic) up to the case duration”
    Each step costs one to two factorizations; increase the step or shorten the duration.
