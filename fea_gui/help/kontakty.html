Contacts

Real contact between assembly parts: separation, sliding with or without friction, press fit (interference), compression only. Without contact pairs touching parts are rigidly joined by a shared mesh (linear). Every contact makes the analysis nonlinear.

Nonlinear analysis: load cases are solved incrementally, superposition does not hold, so no fatigue by superposition. The safety factor is a “stress reserve”, not a load multiplier. Always CalculiX (no NGSolve). Contact stiffness K and stick slope λ are penalty values and μ is an estimate: check sensitivity (two values of μ, another K).

Procedure

  1. Find touching parts: pairs within a distance are classified as contact, gap or penetration. Penetration with a type other than interference is an assembly error (modelled thread, nominal interference): fix the CAD or choose Interference.
  2. Choose the type, Add selected (no selection = all new pairs). A pair not added stays bonded.
  3. Click a pair in the table: faces highlight in 3D (colour = type), values load into the form. Edit type, μ (0 = from the friction library by purpose), K, λ, interference, Save changes.
  4. Pair between selected faces only: side A faces → A from selection, side B faces → B from selection, Add new pair. Smaller faces = faster, more accurate.
  5. In Solve check readiness (e.g. initial penetration, friction without support against sliding), then run.

Nonlinear steps

  • Cases separately (branches) – default: each load case solved separately (after bolt preload), in parallel; no history.
  • Step sequence – steps in one job; contact and friction state is kept (path-dependent). A step has: load case × scale, bolts (preload / locked / free), deactivated pairs, friction change, prescribed motion of a rigid remote support (history in step time), increment control, cycles (fatigue with friction ≥ 2).
  • Prescribed motion (displacement / rotation of a rigid remote support): converges better than force control; gives the force–travel curve (Contacts tab).

Results

  • Contact – pressure, contact status (−1 not a contact node, 0 open, 1 sliding, 2 sticking), opening, slip, frictional stress – best in a section.
  • Contacts tab: normal and frictional force of the pair, contact area, max and mean pressure, opening, slip, numbers of sticking / sliding / open nodes, force–travel curve.
  • Which pressure to trust: the mean pressure from the contact force (p_force = exact pair force from ccx ÷ active area) and the resultant. The peak nodal pressure depends on formulation and mesh: with quadratic elements it scatters even under uniform pressure (press fit: nodes 48–110 MPa for a true 79 MPa), at a free surface edge it grows with refinement, and another program or contact formulation can give a peak several times different for the same joint. So p_max inside the surface (without faces at a free edge) is reported too, and a peak at the edge gives a warning. Mortar as default was tried: smoother press-fit pressure, but zero pressures at surface edges (patch test −6 % of force) and 4–10× slower; surface-to-surface stays.
  • Partial result: without convergence the last converged increment is kept with a red label; advice in the Messages dock. Stop and keep results (Solve) ends the analysis the same way.

Rigid remote load with rotation: without NLGEOM, ccx treats rigid-body rotation as finite, the rest as small. Imbalance grows with θ² (0.06–0.4 % at 0.05 rad; above 0.1 % the analysis ends with an error); the program warns from 0.03 rad. Reduce the rotation, switch NLGEOM on, or use the distributing coupling.

No convergence: use displacement instead of force control (a part held only by friction slides away); reduce the first increment dt₀; switch friction on in a later step (start with μ ≈ 1e-4); check initial gaps and penetrations; add weak springs (Stabilization).
