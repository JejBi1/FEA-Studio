3. 3D printing

Sets the print method for FDM/FFF parts: perimeters, solid layers, infill and build direction. A printed part is analyzed as a shell of solid extrusion around an infill core. The result gives a safety factor (Tsai-Wu or Tsai-Hill) and an indicative creep estimate.

Warning: infill properties, anisotropy ratios, creep parameters and print S-N curves are INDICATIVE (sparse literature data, own estimates). For a critical part calibrate them with your own tensile tests (see Calibration) and treat results as comparative.

How the program models a printed part

A part is printed when a printed material is assigned: separate properties in the layer plane (E, Rm) and normal to the layers (E_z, Rm_z). All material values are for solid extrusion (100 % infill); the infill and shell effect is computed by the program.

  • Shell = solid material at the surface. Vertical walls: perimeters, thickness perimeters × line width (2 × 0.42 = 0.84 mm). Horizontal faces: top and bottom solid layers, layers × layer height (5 × 0.2 = 1.0 mm top, 3 × 0.2 = 0.6 mm bottom). On inclined faces the larger contribution governs. A face touching another part is also a print surface.
  • Core = infill with homogenized properties from the pattern and density (Gibson–Ashby type: E_core = E · k · ρn); single infill ribs are not modeled.
  • Shell fraction per element: 32 points are sampled on the shell boundary of each element (0 = infill, 1 = solid), rounded to tenths. Stiffness is a mix of shell and core with the same strain (isostrain); mass is computed the same way.
  • Build direction (printer Z) gives the material axes: 1 and 2 in the layer plane, 3 normal to the layers (transversely isotropic).
  • Line anisotropy in perimeters: the line runs along the contour, so along the line the material is stiffer and stronger than across it. The line direction is the wall tangent in the layer, rounded to 5°. With r = across/along, the value along the line is 2/(1 + r) · XY and across it r × that. Default PLA (r = 0.55 for strength): Rm = 39 MPa gives 50 MPa along the line and 28 MPa across. Solid top and bottom layers are isotropic in the plane.

Tip: in bending the shell carries most of the load, so more perimeters usually help more than more infill. Change Perimeters and watch the “Shell / Core” line.

Procedure in the step

  1. Select the printed parts in the part list (Ctrl / Shift). The status in brackets shows e.g. [material; 2× perimeter, infill 15 % Grid; Z = +Z], [Steel S235 – not printed] or [bolt / omitted].
  2. Choose the printed material and press Assign.
  3. Enter the slicer parameters and press Apply to selected parts (nothing is saved before).
  4. Choose the Failure criterion. For sustained loads turn on Creep and check the long-term cases.
  5. After meshing (Mesh), check the shell distribution with Shell / core.

Refine mesh at critical locations and recompute

After the analysis, the mesh around the locations with the lowest print safety factor (1–8 locations) is refined in spheres to about the shell thickness and the study is recomputed (two-step refinement). Refining the whole surface would be too expensive. Below the button the min. safety before and after, and node counts are shown; you can then adopt the spheres into the study (Mesh step) and the new results.

If the minimum after refinement lies at a sharp inner edge it is a singularity: the value falls with each refinement and has no finite limit. Add the real corner radius in CAD, or evaluate away from the edge; the program then also states the lowest safety factor outside the singularity.

Print detail (voxels)

Print safety factor and failure index

The strain at the 4 Gauss points of each element is taken from the displacements and converted to print axes (line axes for perimeters). Shell and core share the strain but have stresses σ = C·ε; each is assessed with its own strengths, and the worse governs the element.

  • Safety factor λ (“3D printing – safety factor (Tsai-Wu/Hill)” in Results): the factor by which all loads of the case must be multiplied to reach criterion 1. λ = 2 = failure at twice the load. For Tsai-Wu this is not a simple stress/strength ratio (linear terms differ for tension and compression).
  • Failure index = 1/λ (“3D printing – failure index”): 1 = failure, 0.5 = half utilization.
  • At a node the worst surrounding element is shown, without averaging across material jumps. Non-printed parts show λ = 10 and index 0.
  • The results info panel and the bottom of the step list, per case and part, the min. safety factor, location, region (shell, perimeter (line axes, across, Z) or core (infill)) and σ11 σ22 σ33 τ12 τ13 τ23 in print axes. σ33 shows whether tension between layers governs.

Warning: “Safety factor to Re” does not assess printed parts (plastics have no yield strength); use “3D printing – safety factor”. Each load case is assessed separately; put simultaneous loads in one case. The report rates the print PASS already at λ ≥ 1; judge the margin yourself (for uncalibrated data rather 2–3).

Calibration from ISO 527 tests

Specimens have a shell and infill like parts. The Printed material calibration – ISO 527 dialog back-calculates the solid extrusion with the same model the analysis uses, so parts with other print settings match.

How to test

  • ISO 527-2 type 1A/1B specimens (gauge b = 10 mm, h = 4 mm). xy = lying flat (tension in the layer plane); z = standing upright (tension normal to layers).
  • Print with 100 % infill if possible; at least 3 specimens per direction (the dialog gives the coefficient of variation).
  • Measure modulus with an extensometer; crosshead travel includes machine and grip compliance and gives a much lower modulus.

What is computed

For each specimen the load-bearing fraction is φ = (A_perimeters · c + A_solid layers + A_core · k)/A, where k is the infill ratio in the direction and c = 2/(1 + r) (perimeters of an XY specimen are loaded along the line; r = across/along line ratio; c = 1 for Z specimens). Hence the solid E_xy comes out slightly lower than the measured average, and the analysis then returns exactly the measured stiffness. Line ratios of the default material (Apply to material) are inherited. Solid extrusion: E_solid = E_meas/φ_E and R_solid = Rm_meas/φ_R, averaged per direction.

What the estimate adds to the results

  • Each long-term case is recomputed on the same mesh with E_c = k_E·E/(1 + k_φ·φ(t)) and strengths × k_R. Results gain a case “<case> – creep 1000 h, 30 °C” with all quantities; its print safety factor refers to the long-term strength.
  • The bottom of the 3D printing step and the report summarize: max. displacement short-term → long-term and their ratio, short-term utilization, and the min. long-term safety factor.
  • Metal parts do not creep, so the estimate shows the redistribution of forces between rigid and creeping parts. For a single-material model the long-term displacement is the short-term one divided by the stiffness factor and stresses do not change.

Limits of the estimate

  • Linear viscoelasticity holds up to ~30–35 % of strength. If short-term print utilization (1/λ) exceeds 35 %, a warning says real creep will be larger: the estimate is non-conservative.
  • The load must be constant and the temperature uniform. Humidity and ageing are not included.
  • Relaxation of bolt preload in plastic is not computed (see Joints).

Warning: every study with a printed part shows a warning, and the Word report always contains the red notice “WARNING – CREEP: the linear model does not capture plastic creep…”. If long-term cases were not computed, the report says so (“long-term cases were not computed (3D printing step → Creep)”); the summary then assesses the print without creep. The notice cannot be turned off – it is a deliberate limitation of the method.

Fatigue of printed parts

Set up in Fatigue as for metals, with a print-specific procedure:

  • Stress is computed in print axes (line axes for perimeters), separately for shell and core. Multiaxial stress becomes a signed Hill equivalent; directional fatigue strengths = static strengths × SD/Rm.
  • Then as for metals: rainflow, mean-stress correction (Goodman/Gerber with Rm XY), Miner's rule; a node takes the worst surrounding element.
  • S-N curve from the Perimeters, creep and fatigue box (default Ezeh & Susmel). Marin factors are not used (test specimens already contain print defects). An S-N curve entered in Fatigue applies to XY.
  • Limits: one slope for all directions; compressive amplitudes taken as tensile (conservative); no frequency effect; no notch sensitivity of the print.

Variants, sweep and advice – how to strengthen or lighten a part

Below the settings in the 3D printing step: the Internal structure variants group and two collapsible sections, ▸ Parameter sweep and ▸ Advice by failure location and build direction.

Print settings do not change the mesh: all variants run on a single mesh (no remeshing) and differ only in shell and infill assignment. Each variant reports the min. print safety factor (and where), max. displacement and printed mass.

Variants

A variant = named overrides per part (perimeters, solid layers, infill, pattern, line, layer, build direction, material). Compute variants runs the study settings and all variants; the recommended one is the lightest with safety factor ≥ the required one (default 1.5). Adopt the selected variant into the study.

Parameter sweep

Enter perimeters, infill densities, patterns and optionally build directions; all combinations run on one mesh (at most 60 variants). Separate values by commas or spaces (“2, 3, 4”); with a decimal comma use semicolons (“7,5; 15”). Estimate shows variant count and time. The mass × safety plot shows the Pareto front (no other variant beats it in both mass and safety). The recommended variant is green, Pareto points ★; clicking the plot selects the table row. Adopt selected variant applies it and recomputes; Add it to variants keeps it.

Advice by failure location

From the weakest location of each part (perimeter, solid layers, infill, thin region) and stress components in print axes, the program suggests: stress along the perimeter line → more perimeters; infill governs → higher density (gyroid for multi-directional loading); loaded layer bond (σ33, τ13, τ23) → different build direction. Each advice can be tried at once (Try as variant); for infill, Sweep infill prefills the sweep.

Warning: advice is INDICATIVE – verify the benefit with a variant (analysis) and, for important parts, a test.

Rank build directions

Computes the part built along all six axes ±X / ±Y / ±Z and ranks them by print safety factor. Adopt the best with one click (other settings stay).

Presets

Save print settings (layer, line, perimeters, top/bottom layers, infill, pattern, thin regions) under a name and apply them to other parts; the build direction stays. Only “3D print standard” is built in. Presets are stored in %APPDATA%\FEAStudio\print_presets.json.

Solid infill of thin regions

Where the interior after subtracting perimeters is narrower than the limit (default 2 × line width), sparse infill does not fit and slicers print solid; the program does the same. The limit can be entered or the option turned off. Indicative – slicers decide by region area.

In-plane anisotropy of infills

Grid, rectilinear and zig-zag infills have lines at ±45° to the print axes: same modulus in print axes, but stiffer in in-plane shear (indicatively after Gibson and Ashby). Gyroid, cubic, honeycomb and triangles are approximately isotropic in the plane.

Recommendations

  • Orientation on the bed: the principal tensile stress should lie in the layer plane. Layers normal to it are the weakest (typically 50–70 % of XY strength, less for non-annealed PLA) and fail in a brittle way. Rotate bent arms and hooks so the bending plane lies in the layers.
  • Bolt holes with an axis parallel to the build direction are surrounded by perimeters running around the contour – best for bearing and tension.
  • Mesh and shell: the shell is usually 0.6–1.3 mm and elements 2–4 mm, so shell stress depends on the mesh. At critical places (eye, notch, rib root) refine locally in Mesh, ideally to about the shell thickness. Do not refine the whole surface: for thin parts it means hundreds of thousands of nodes and tens of GB of solver memory.
  • Check the Shell / core section. Thin walls (≤ 2 × perimeter thickness) are fully solid, as in real printing.
  • For critical parts calibrate the properties and verify with a test of the part.
  • Printed parts can also use the large-model solver (NGSolve); results are identical to CalculiX.

Common problems

  • “3D printing 'part': elements at the surface (~2.5 mm) are much larger than the shell (0.6 mm) – shell stiffness is only averaged…”
    Elements are over 3× the thinnest shell. Stiffness is usually fine, but shell stress depends on the mesh. Define local refinement at the minimum-safety location in Mesh and repeat; keep refining while the minimum changes significantly.
  • “Creep 'material': temperature 50 °C is outside the validity of the estimate (up to 45 °C, near the glass transition) – the linear model cannot be used”
    Near PLA's glass transition (55–60 °C) the linear estimate is invalid. Lower the Part temperature, or choose a higher-Tg material (PETG up to 60 °C, ABS/ASA up to 70 °C).
  • “Creep '…': short-term print utilization 48 % > 35 % – outside linear viscoelasticity…”
    The permanent load exceeds the linear-creep limit and the estimate is non-conservative. Enlarge the section, add perimeters or change orientation until utilization is below 35 %.
  • “Creep is enabled, but no load case is marked as long-term”
    Tick the continuously acting cases in the Long-term cases list. After a rename the tick carries over; after deleting and recreating a case it does not.
  • “Part '…' has print settings, but its material is not a printed material (E_z missing) – treated as isotropic”
    An ordinary material was assigned later in Materials. Assign a printed material here, or ignore the print settings.
  • “Generate the mesh first (Mesh step).” after Shell / core
    The section needs a mesh. “No part has a printed material.” = no meshed part has a material with E_z.
  • Calibration: “The curve does not reach a strain of 0.25 %” / “The file does not contain two numeric columns (force, elongation)”
    Check L0 (0 only if the second column is strain in %) and the file format. “the infill carries a large part of the cross-section (φ_E < 0.6)” means the result depends on the infill estimate: print the specimens with 100 % infill.
