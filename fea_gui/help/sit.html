Mesh

Gmsh generates quadratic tetrahedra tet10 (C3D10 in CalculiX). Mesh fineness sets the stress accuracy and the analysis time.

Procedure

  1. Enter the Element size (largest element edge, mm).
  2. Where stresses matter (eye, notch, weld), add Local refinement: select faces in 3D, click Refine selection.
  3. Click Generate mesh; check the node and element counts and the min. scaled Jacobian below the button and in the log.

The Solve step meshes by itself; generating here is only for checking. Any change of geometry, selections, materials, joints, print settings or mesh discards the mesh (orange dot) and the results.

Settings not in the panel

The mesh section of study.json (in the .fea project) has more items; do not change them normally:

  • order = 2 – quadratic tet10 (tet4 is much too stiff in bending).
  • optimize_high_order = true – optimizes curved elements only when min. scaled Jacobian is below 0.2 (otherwise up to 70 % of the meshing time).
  • algorithm3d = 10 – HXT (about 2× faster than Delaunay); on failure Delaunay (1) is used with a warning.
  • threads = 0 – one Gmsh thread, so the mesh is repeatable. Higher values are faster but the mesh differs between runs.

Mesh convergence

In Results: Mesh convergence… (finer meshes, Richardson, GCI) and the Stress error estimate quantity (above ~10 % refine there). For 3D printing use the refinement button in 3D printing.

Separately meshed parts (*TIE)

Parts touching along a face share nodes. If that fails (tangent contact, “PLC Error: segment and facet intersect”) or a part needs its own mesh, list it in Joints → Parts meshed separately; it is tied to its neighbours by *TIE (listed in the table below the list and in Messages).

Mesh quality

The scaled Jacobian (SJ): 1 = ideal, 0 = flat, negative = inverted (unusable).

  • min. SJ ≥ 0.2 – fine.
  • 0 < min. SJ < 0.2 – curved elements are optimized (log warning “Curved elements min. SJ … – higher-order optimization”).
  • min. SJ ≤ 0 after optimization – the mesh is regenerated with straight edges; holes and pins become facets and their stresses are less accurate. Reduce the element size or increase Elements on circle.

How to choose the element size

  • At least 2, preferably 3 tet10 elements through a bent wall (6 mm plate → 2–3 mm). Fewer elements underestimate bending stress.
  • At the evaluated location (fillet, hole, notch): at least 3–4 elements per fillet radius, by local refinement.
  • Convergence: refine the decisive location 2× and compare; a change below 5 % is sufficient. If stress keeps growing, it is a singularity (sharp corner, support edge) – see Results.
  • Size: halving the element size means about 8× more elements and DOF (DOF ≈ 5 × tet10 elements).

Printed parts

A printed part has a stiff shell (perimeters, solid layers) and an infill core. The shell fraction is computed per element, so the mesh need not follow the shell. Shell thickness = perimeters × line width (3 × 0.42 = 1.26 mm) and solid layers × layer height (3 × 0.2 = 0.6 mm).

  • A surface element larger than 3× the thinnest shell triggers the warning “3D printing '…': elements at the surface (…) are much larger than the shell…”. Stiffness is fine, shell stress depends on the mesh.
  • Solution: local refinement only at critical places (eye, arm root) to 1–1.5 mm. Do not refine the whole surface: an 80×20×12 mm bracket with 0.6 mm elements had 507 thousand nodes and needed 15 GB in CalculiX.

Assemblies: common (conforming) mesh

Parts touching along a face in CAD are rigidly connected (as if glued); common nodes are created on their contact faces.

  • A gap (even 0.01 mm) means no connection. Without a bolt, weld or support, the parts are free bodies and the analysis fails.
  • Parts touching only along an edge or point share a few nodes – unrealistic. Check Connected parts (shared mesh) in Joints.
  • Bolts are not meshed (a beam replaces them); bearing faces under head and nut are refined to 0.25 × d (M6 → 1.5 mm). A weld is meshed with element size a (if smaller than the global size).
  • Parts excluded in Joints are not meshed.
  • Contact pairs (Contacts) are meshed separately (non-conforming, needed for interference), or conformally with a split interface (bolted joints). No *TIE is created. Changing pairs, sides or interface meshing needs a new mesh; changing μ, K, λ or interference does not.

In the examples

Example 1 – printed bracket

  • Element size 2.5 mm, no local refinement, Elements on circle 12 (Ø5 screw holes ≈ 1.3 mm, Ø8 eye ≈ 2.1 mm).
  • The 6 mm plate has 2–3 elements through the thickness, the 8 mm arm about 3.
  • The thinnest shell layer is 0.6 mm (bottom layers), so a 2.5 mm element triggers the coarse-mesh warning. Refine the eye and arm root to 1–1.5 mm and compare the print safety factor.

Example 2 – assembly

  • Element size 4 mm, local refinement Arm eye: 1.5 mm.
  • Base plate 10 mm and arm flange 8 mm have 2–3 elements through the thickness.
  • Under the M6 bolt heads and nuts the mesh is refined to 1.5 mm automatically; the a4 welds keep the 4 mm global element.
  • Base plate, post, welds and arm share one mesh; bolts and welds provide the connection.

Troubleshooting

Message / symptomCause and solution
“The volume mesh was not generated – the geometry probably self-intersects or parts touch tangentially … Gmsh: …”Overlapping or tangent parts (pin in a slot, cylinder on a plane). Fix the geometry (clearance or a clear face contact), or exclude the part in Joints.
“Parts 'A' and 'B' overlap (volume …); overlap assigned to part 'A'”Interference in CAD (bolt without a hole, pressed-in pin). The analysis runs, but the common volume belongs to one part. Fix the model.
“Selection '…': after fragmentation found X of Y mm²” or “Some selected faces were not found”The face was split when joining, or hidden under another part. Select the free part of the face, or restore it with Faces from selection in Supports / Loads.
“Selection '…': face is not on the body surface”A contact face of two parts is now inside the assembly. Put the support or load on an outer face.
“Curved elements with a non-positive Jacobian…; mesh regenerated with straight edges”Elements too large on small radii. Reduce Element size, increase Elements on circle or refine locally.
Hundreds of thousands of elementsSmall chamfers, threads, logos refine the mesh. Increase Min. size, decrease Elements on circle, or remove the details in CAD.
