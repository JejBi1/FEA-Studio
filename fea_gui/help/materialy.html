2. Materials

Assigns materials from the built-in or your own library to parts, edits their properties, and converts steel and alloy designations between standards (ČSN, EN, W.Nr., DIN, AISI…).

Procedure

  1. Select parts in the parts list (Ctrl / Shift = several), or click parts in 3D (Ctrl+click adds).
  2. Choose a material in the drop-down list – its properties load into Properties. Or type the designation from the drawing into the Converter, which selects the matching material.
  3. Optionally edit the properties (for a modified material also change the Name).
  4. Assign to selected, or Assign to all.

The parts list shows each part's material in square brackets. A part without its own assignment uses the default material of the study – Steel S235 in a new study; Assign to all sets a new default.

Printed materials

“Print …” materials are orthotropic: E and Rm apply in the layer plane (XY), the rest perpendicular to the layers. Below the form: “Printed material (E, Rm = XY layer plane): E_z … MPa, Rm_z … MPa”. E_z, ν_xz, G between layers, Rm_z and compressive and shear strengths are edited in the 3D printing step (“Printed material – solid extrusion (100 % infill)”) and kept when editing here.

Orthotropic material

Three mutually perpendicular symmetry axes (wood, composite, laminate). In Material properties… tick Orthotropic material (axes 1, 2, 3) and enter:

ConstantsMeaning
E1, E2, E3 [MPa]Moduli along axes 1, 2, 3 (E and ν above are then unused; E = E1, ν = ν12).
ν12, ν13, ν23Poisson's ratios: εj = −νij·σi/Ei (CalculiX / Abaqus convention).
G12, G13, G23 [MPa]Shear moduli.
α1, α2, α3 [10⁻⁶/K]Optional thermal expansion along the axes; isotropic = α of the material.

Material axes come from a coordinate system (Systems…, as for loads): global axes (1, 2, 3 = x, y, z), a local Cartesian system, or cylindrical (1 = radial, 2 = tangential, 3 = axial – pipes, wound parts, trunks). Fill isotropically from E, ν prefills the constants. The constants must give a positive-definite matrix – checked in Solve.

Note: the safety factor to Re and von Mises do not apply to orthotropic materials – evaluate stress components in material axes against their strengths (results are in global axes). Orthotropic materials are solved by CalculiX (automatic choice). For 3D printing use 3D printing.

Metals for 3D Printing (L-PBF)

Laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF, formerly SLM / DMLS). The program treats them as isotropic: the 3D printing step (perimeters, infill) is meant for FDM plastics, so a metal part is printed solid. Printed metal properties depend strongly on heat treatment (as-built has residual stress and, for Ti-6Al-4V, low ductility), on the build direction (usually lower in Z; for 17-4PH the XY plane is weaker) and on the machine. The library takes the lowest value of the cited sources for Re and Rm (usually Z); the name gives the condition: as-built = no heat treatment; annealed / stress-relieved / aged / hardened / HIP = after that treatment.

Plasticity (elastoplastic metal)

In the Material properties window tick Plasticity (elastoplastic metal) to compute the material beyond the yield point (von Mises yield condition with hardening). The study then runs as a nonlinear CalculiX analysis (no superposition of load cases, no NGSolve) and the results get the equivalent plastic strain PEEQ.

The window checks the definition at once (the same rules as the readiness check: missing Re, Et ≥ E, a decreasing table, K ≤ Re… are errors) and draws the tensile curve the solver gets (true stress – total strain). A material with plasticity is saved to your library with it.

Caution: the curve is the main source of uncertainty – use true stress – plastic strain data of the actual material and condition. No damage, rupture, strain-rate or temperature dependence is modelled.

Friction between materials

The friction coefficient belongs to a pair of surfaces, not to one material, so the form does not have it. For contacts (μ = 0 in the pair form = from the library) and joint friction in mechanisms the program takes μ from the friction library by material pair and purpose: for force transmitted by friction (joint slip, press fit) the lower bound μ_min; for mechanism resistance the upper bound μ_max. Values and indicative damping: Friction and damping; your own measured values go to Tools → Calculators… → Friction library.

Your material library

File %APPDATA%\FEAStudio\materials.json. Save to my material library adds the form's material; a material with the same name is replaced. Built-in materials cannot be overwritten. Your materials are offered in all studies; a study keeps its own copy of the materials it uses, so it opens on a computer without your library.

Grade converter

Enter the designation in any system – ČSN (11 373, 12 050.1, 17 240), EN (S235JR, C45E), W.Nr. (1.0503), DIN (St 52-3, Ck45), AISI/SAE, UNS, ASTM, JIS, KS, GB, GOST, PN, AFNOR, BS. It also knows the new library materials (1.4542 / 17-4 PH, 100Cr6 / AISI 52100, 1.2379 / AISI D2, CuZn39Pb3 / CW614N) and the metals for 3D printing (2.4856 / Inconel 625, 1.2344 / H13, Co28Cr6Mo, CuCrZr / CW106C, Scalmalloy). It shows:

  • the material group and family (“approximate match only” = not exact, marked “≈”);
  • designations in other standards, and possibly other groups (“Also:”);
  • In library: an identical library material (selected at once), or similar ones with the difference (composition, Rm) – a click selects it;
  • the number of S-N records in the PragTic database (for the Fatigue step).

Note: the converter maps designations, not properties of a particular heat. What counts are the standard values for your condition and thickness (yield strength falls with thickness; +N / +QT / +C differ).

Editing and a new material

  • Select the closest material, edit the values, change the Name (e.g. “S355 t = 40 mm”) and assign.
  • Assigning under an existing name overwrites the study material for all parts that use it.
  • Materials no part uses after the assignment (and that are not the default) are removed from the study.
  • To keep it for other studies, use Save to my material library.

Tip: assigning a material does not delete the other study materials (a calibrated printed material, materials of print variants); unused materials are not listed in the report.

In the examples

  • Example 1 – printed bracket: the only part has Print PLA Bambu Basic (the study default). The form shows E_z 2 160 MPa and Rm_z 35 MPa; shell, infill and build direction are in the 3D printing step. Re is not specified, so the safety factor is taken from “3D printing – safety factor”, not “Safety factor to Re”.
  • Example 2 – assembly: base plate, post and welds have the default Steel S235; the arm has Print PLA Bambu Basic. Both have λ, α and c_p, so steady and transient thermal cases and thermal stress all run. Bolts take their strength class (8.8) from the part name, not from the material.

Tip: type “11 375” into the Converter – Steel S235 is selected; “12050” shows the group C45 (1.0503, AISI 1045, S45C…) and selects Steel C45 +N.

Troubleshooting

  • “Part '…' has no material assigned” – no own and no default material (script-built study or hand-edited study.json). Select the part and Assign to selected, or Assign to all.
  • “Material '…' (part '…') has no thermal conductivity k [W/(m·K)]” / “… has no density or specific heat c_p (required for a transient case)” – the study has a thermal case. Fill in λ (and for transient ρ and c_p) and assign again.
  • “Material '…' (part '…') has no coefficient of thermal expansion α [1/K] (thermal load)” – fill in α (in 10⁻⁶/K) and assign.
  • “A built-in material cannot be overwritten – choose a different name.” – save to your library under your own name.
  • “The user material library … cannot be read …” – materials.json is corrupted (manual editing). The program does not overwrite it, so no data is lost; fix or rename it.
  • Safety factor to Re of a plastic part is 10 everywhere – the material has no Re. For printed parts use “3D printing – safety factor”; for other plastics enter Re (yield or allowable stress) in your own material.
  • “Spectral fatigue: material without Rm and without S-N (…) – fatigue is not computed” – fill in Rm (S-N estimate) or enter an S-N curve in Fatigue; see Dynamics.
