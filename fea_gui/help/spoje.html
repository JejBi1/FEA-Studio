4. Joints (bolts, welds)

The Joints step marks which parts are bolts and welds and sets their parameters. In the analysis a bolt is a beam with preload. After the analysis the bolts are checked per VDI 2230 and EN 1993-1-8, the welds per EN 1993-1-8, EN 1993-1-9 or IIW, from the FEM forces.

Tip: set up joints before meshing. Changing bolt size, weld throat or disabling discards mesh and results; assessment-only changes (FAT, γ, μ, filler metal) keep the FEM results.

Joint detection

Detect joints…

Scans the parts and offers the joints found in the Detected joints – confirmation dialog; accepted joints are added to the existing ones.

  • By name (confidence 1.0): “ISO 4017 - M8 x 30 - 8.8”, “ISO 4762 - M6 x 20 - 12.9”, “ISO 4032 - M8 - 8”, “ISO 7089 - 8,4 - 200 HV”, including the Fusion suffix “:1”. A name without a standard (e.g. “Sroub M4 x 17.25”) works with a keyword or M d × L; the head type then comes from the shape.
  • By shape: a part without a standard name with a coaxial shank and head (hex, cylindrical, countersunk), nut or washer; dimensions are compared with ISO tables. Confidence is below 1 and such proposals are pre-checked only at ≥ 0.7. Pins, magnets, bushings and parts with a middle collar are not bolts.
  • Welds: a name with “svar”, “weld” or “W_…”, a CAD layer or a “weld/svar” attribute, or a colour chosen in Weld color in CAD (tolerance 0.03 in RGB 0–1).

Bolt into selected hole…

For a model without bolts: select the hole's cylindrical face in 3D and press the button. The largest bolt whose ISO 273 fine-series clearance hole fits is proposed, with strength class 8.8 by default. All parts coaxial with the hole are clamped.

Virtual bolts from a CAD assembly

Opening an Inventor assembly .iam directly (module native): fasteners recognized by name (e.g. Content Center “ISO 4762 M6 x 20”, “ISO 4032 M6”, “ISO 7089 6”) are not loaded as bodies and are replaced virtually: size, length, standard and class from the name, axis and bearing face from the occurrence, clamped parts from coaxial holes. A bolt into a thread is a hole smaller than d in the last part; a through hole without a nut → ISO 4032 nut assumed (warning). A missing class is taken as 8.8 – check it in the bolt form.

How a bolt is modeled

  • Bolt, nut and washers are not meshed. The bolt is a beam (Timoshenko, element U1 in CalculiX) on the hole axis between the head and nut bearing faces; its stiffness is the VDI 2230 compliance of bolt and washers.
  • The beam ends connect by rigid coupling to the annulus under the head and nut (outer diameter of the bearing face), imprinted on the part face during meshing. For a bolt into a thread, the end connects to the hole's cylindrical face over the engaged length. The bearing face is rigid (VDI assumption).
  • The mesh around bolts is refined to 0.25 × d.
  • Preload: the beam is cut in the middle. If the “Bolt preload” case is enabled, a load case “Bolt preload” closes the cut by force F_V; in the other cases the cut is closed and the bolt carries only the service load. Cases can be superposed.
  • Countersunk head (ISO 10642) connects to the conical countersink surface. Slot instead of a hole = two half-cylinders.
  • Fitted bolt (ISO 7379, DIN 609, 7968, 610) transfers transverse force through the shank: shank shear and bearing with the shank diameter. Slip is informative only.
  • Bolt into a thread (no nut) also gets a thread stripping check.
  • Gap between clamped parts in CAD → clamped stack: intermediate parts are connected by an imprint and the gap is taken as closed. The bolt share is Φ_n = 0.5·Φ_K, bolt bending from FEM is not assessed and the preload case is off for that bolt (otherwise a mechanism).
  • Bolts and welds always run in CalculiX, even for large models.

Warning: parts touching over a face are rigidly bonded after meshing (shared nodes, no contact). Forces at the interface (axial F_A, transverse F_Q) come from this bonded area around each bolt; the VDI check verifies that the interface neither opens nor slips.

Bolted joint interface as contact

To allow real opening or slip, check bolted joint interfaces as contact in Contacts. The clamped interface of each bolt becomes contact with friction μ = μ_T (this step), with a conformal split interface, and the analysis becomes nonlinear (always CalculiX). The preload acts in step 1; later steps lock the bolt. The bolt becomes a connector (rigid bearing faces and springs: axial E·A/L, bending and torsional stiffness of the shank). The result adds the residual clamp force from contact and the open fraction of the interface; slip shows as contact status 1. Verified by K6 (M10: clamp force from contact = F_V; at slip F_t = μ_T·F_n).

Contact makes the analysis larger and cancels load-case superposition. For a usual VDI 2230 check the bonded interface is enough.

How a weld is evaluated

  • The weld body is meshed with the parts, refined to about size a. Forces are summed from integration-point stresses by segments along the root; nominal stresses in the section a·L give σ⊥, τ⊥, τ∥.
  • EN 1993-1-8 directional method: √(σ⊥² + 3(τ⊥² + τ∥²)) ≤ f_u/(β_w·γ_M2) and σ⊥ ≤ 0.9·f_u/γ_M2; β_w from yield. A full-penetration butt weld is assessed as base material (σ_eq ≤ f_y).
  • Fatigue (with a load history in Fatigue): ranges σ_w and τ_w, Miner D = D_σ + D_τ ≤ 1, and the toe separately. The “D fatigue” column shows the worse of root and toe.
  • Design warnings: a < 3 mm, a > 0.7·t_min, length < max(30 mm; 6a).
  • Intermittent weld: parallel fillet welds between the same parts on one line form a group; gaps ≤ min(12t; 200 mm) (compression, shear) and ≤ min(16t; 200 mm) (tension) are checked for information (EN 1993-1-8, 4.3.2.1). Each segment is still assessed alone.

Parts excluded from the analysis

Excluded parts are not meshed – for magnets, pins, cables or free parts. They show transparent in 3D. Use Exclude the part of the selected face after clicking its face; Restore brings it back. If the analysis reports “free bodies”, the program offers to exclude the unconnected parts.

Parts meshed separately (*TIE connection)

A checked part gets its own independent mesh, and after meshing it is tied to its neighbours by a *TIE constraint (rigid connection without shared nodes). Use it when Gmsh does not join the parts (tangent contact, “PLC Error”) or when a part needs a different mesh density.

Connected parts (shared mesh)

The table (Part A, Part B, Contact [mm²]) is filled after meshing and lists pairs rigidly bonded by shared nodes; clicking a row highlights both parts. Example 2 “Assembly”

Detection by name finds:

  • Bolt 1, Bolt 2: ISO 4017 M6×30 8.8 with ISO 4032 nut and two ISO 7089 washers. Clamped: arm flange (PLA, 8 mm) and base plate (S235, 10 mm), l_K = 21.2 mm, holes Ø6.6 (ISO 273 medium). The check warns that embedding and creep of plastic are not covered by VDI 2230.
  • Weld 1, Weld 2: “Weld a4 left/right”, fillet a = 4 mm over 70 mm, column to base plate. Separate contact of welded parts makes the lateral force on the column go entirely through the welds.

Compare the bolts (cases “Force on arm eye”, “Heating in service”, “Bolt preload”) and welds (250 N lateral force at the column head gives bending and σ⊥ at the root) in the utilization dock. Joint fatigue uses two sine channels 90° apart in Fatigue. After changing the history, press Re-check.

Common problems

  • “Bolt 1: there is a 0.5 mm gap between the head and part 'X' – the head does not seat”, “the nut does not seat on part …”
    Fix the mates in CAD and export STEP again. “no coaxial hole or slot found in part 'X'” = hole off the bolt axis or missing.
  • “Bolts cannot be connected to the mesh: Bolt 1: constraint face '…' has no elements in the mesh”
    Bearing-face imprint not created (head does not seat, or hole larger than the bearing diameter). A bolt into a thread needs a cylindrical thread face.
  • “Some parts are not connected to the supports (free bodies – the problem would be singular)”
    The part touches nothing over a face and has no bolt or weld. Exclude it, make the parts touch, or add a bolt.
  • “The volume mesh was not created – the geometry probably intersects itself or the parts touch tangentially”
    Tangent contact (pin in a slot, cylinder on a plane). Exclude the part, or remove the tangency in CAD.
  • “The weld does not touch any part!”, “Length 20.0 mm < max(30 mm; 6a) – the weld should not be counted”
    The weld body must lie with a face on both welded parts. EN 1993-1-8 does not count a short weld as load-carrying.
  • “The load history does not match the load cases – joint fatigue not assessed”
    After adding or deleting a load case, update the channels in Fatigue and press Re-check.
