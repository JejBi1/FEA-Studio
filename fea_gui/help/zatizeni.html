Loads

Load cases and the loads in them: force on faces, edges or points (also in local or cylindrical systems), force normal to a face, pressure (constant, linear, hydrostatic), moment and force at a remote point, bearing load, gravity, rotation (centrifugal force) and temperature. Each load case is a separate analysis with the same supports.

Load cases

  • One case = one analysis step (one *STEP in CalculiX). Loads within a case add up; cases are not combined – each has its own results.
  • Cases are also unit cases for fatigue: the load history scales and superposes them (the analysis is linear). For 3D-print creep the “long-term cases” are chosen in 3D printing.
  • For a combination (e.g. force + gravity) put both loads in one case.
  • An empty case gives a warning; with thermal cases an empty load case is skipped. With bolt preload the program adds the Bolt preload case itself – this name is reserved.

Force on an edge and at a point

  • Edge: filter E; a click adds an edge (another click removes it; click on empty space clears). The force spreads uniformly along the length of all selected edges.
  • Point: filter V; a click on a vertex selects it exactly, a click on a face selects a point projected onto the CAD face. Meshing inserts the point as a node; each point gets F/n.

  • Caution: a force on an edge or at a point is singular in a solid model – stress and displacement grow without bound under refinement. Evaluate away from the application point (maxima table marks it). For a real contact (washer, blade) load a small face or use a remote force.

Local and cylindrical coordinate system

  • Systems…: name, type, origin, z axis and x axis (θ = 0). From selection takes the axis of a selected cylindrical face or circle (Edge filter), or the normal of a planar face.
  • Cartesian only rotates the components – same direction everywhere.
  • Cylindrical: (F_r; F_θ; F_z) = radially away from the axis, tangentially (counter-clockwise about z), along the axis. The direction changes over the face; the entered value is the sum of magnitudes (density F/A, F/L), so the resultant is generally not F. A tangential force around a whole shaft circumference gives torque F_θ·R; over a shaft end face ⅔·F_θ·R; a radial inward force over a shell acts like pressure F_r / A.
  • A point on the axis has no radial or tangential direction – such a force ends with an error.

Variable pressure

  • linear: p = p₀ + gradient · (x − origin); gradient [MPa/mm] is a vector (e.g. (0; 0; −0.001) = 1 kPa drop per mm of height). Values may become negative (tension).
  • hydrostatic (liquid): p = p₀ + ρ·g·depth below the level; p₀ = gauge pressure of the gas above the level. Liquid level – point = any point on the level; Gravity direction (0; 0; −1) for Z up, g = 9.81 m/s². Water, 1 m column = 9.81 kPa = 0.00981 MPa.
  • Enters the analysis as consistent nodal forces (exact integration on curved faces; elements cut by the level integrated more finely).

Rotation

  • Static analysis in the rotating frame (d'Alembert): centrifugal force ρ·ω²·r from the speed, and with an angular acceleration α the inertial force −ρ·α × r (positive α = spin-up counter-clockwise about the axis; the drive supplies J·α as a reaction).
  • Rotates: checked parts (e.g. the rotor in an assembly); nothing = all parts. Materials need ρ.
  • No Coriolis force and no centrifugal softening (linear) – valid for ordinary speeds and small deformations. At most one rotation per case.

Inertia from a mechanism

  • Created by Transfer to FEA… in Motion: for the selected instant (or a maximum) the body gets a case with the joint reactions (remote forces, distributing coupling to the joint faces) and an Inertia load – body force −ρ·(a₀ + α × r + ω × (ω × r)) from the body's acceleration, angular velocity and acceleration (d'Alembert); gravity is included. One joint is the support (remote support); its reaction should equal the MBD one.
  • Transferring the whole history creates unit cases (joint reaction and inertia components) and history channels for fatigue.
  • The list shows the description (a₀, ω, α, point, parts); it is not edited in the form – repeat the transfer after changing the mechanism. Added mass (Dynamics) is included.

Remote point: distributing or rigid coupling

  • distributing (adds no stiffness): force and moment are distributed to the face nodes (like RBE3 / Distributed in Ansys); the face deforms freely. Resultant and moment are exact. Recommended.
  • rigid: the face moves as a rigid body (RBE2 / Rigid). It stiffens the model and causes stress peaks at the face edge. Always solved by CalculiX.

Bearing load

A pin in a hole (Bearing Load): the component perpendicular to the axis acts as pressure p ~ cos θ only on the half of the cylinder facing the force; the resultant is exactly F and the moment about the axis zero. The axial component is a uniform shear over the face. F is the force the pin exerts on the part (for a hole, it pushes the wall in the direction of F; for a pin, on the side facing away from F). The selection must be one cylinder – the Hole filter H selects the whole hole. In 3D, 7 arrows show the cosine distribution.

Temperature (thermal stress)

  • thermal case: … – temperature field from Heat (transient: the snapshot at the given time, or end of case). The thermal case is computed first, automatically.
  • uniform part temperature – T [°C] on checked parts (nothing = all); it takes precedence over the thermal field.
  • Stress from ΔT = T − Reference (assembly) temperature (Heat, default 20 °C). Materials need α.
  • At most one thermal field per load case. The load is linear in ΔT, so superposition and fatigue apply.

Member loads

For frame structures (Members) the Type menu adds:

  • Member – nodal force / moment: node (menu or click in 3D), F [N] and M [N·mm] in global axes.
  • Member – distributed load: member (menu or click), q [N/mm] along the length, optionally linearly to a value at the end; in global axes (snow, wind) or local member axes (x along the axis, z = “up” of the section). On an arc, along arc length.
  • Gravity also acts on members (density × section area).

In 3D, member loads are red arrows (size by value).

Display and editing

  • Load faces are red (pressure purple) with a label; a tensile force arrow starts on the face, a compressive one ends on it. A remote force has its arrow at the remote point, a moment a double arrow. Loaded edges are magenta with arrows; points carry a cross; a cylindrical force has arrows at points far from the axis. The rotation axis is a magenta segment.
  • Clicking a list row selects its faces and loads its values; press Save changes to selected after editing.
  • Every load change discards the results; run the analysis again.

Tip: choosing Gravity fills (0; 0; −9810) mm/s²; switching from gravity to another type zeroes the vector. Editing an existing gravity load keeps its value.

In the examples

  • Example 1 – printed bracket: case “Eye load 200 N” = bearing load (0; 0; −200) N on hole “Eye” Ø8 in the boss. The eye axis is horizontal (Y) and the force is perpendicular → cosine pressure on the lower half. Case “Self-weight” = gravity (0; 0; −9810) mm/s². Fatigue scales “Eye load” as pulsating 0–1; creep takes it as long-term.
  • Example 2 – assembly: “Force on the arm eye” – bearing load (0; 0; −300) N on the Ø10 eye, horizontal X axis: cosine pressure on the lower half. “Side force on the post” – force at a remote point (250; 0; 0) N at (35; 35; 150) mm, distributing coupling to the post top face (z = 90 mm): the face carries 250 N and moment 250 N × 60 mm = 15 000 N·mm about Y. “Self-weight” and “Heating in operation” – temperature from thermal case “Operation – steady state” (ΔT relative to 20 °C). The results also contain the case “Bolt preload”.

Common problems

  • “First select one or more faces in the 3D view.” / “Create a load case first.” – select faces, or create a case with +.
  • “A force normal to the face can only be applied to planar faces with the same normal.” – on a curved face use Pressure (p = F / A), or a force in components.
  • “A cylindrical coordinate system is only available for a force …” – for remote load, bearing load and gravity use a Cartesian system or global axes.
  • “Selection '…': point is not on the surface” (meshing) – the point is too far from the surface; select it again with the Point filter on a face or vertex.
  • “… the point lies on the axis of the cylindrical system …” – radial / tangential components on the axis are undefined; move the point or the axis, or use only the axial component.
  • “Selection '…': the bearing load cannot be distributed – the face does not cover the side of the cylinder facing the force” – only part of the hole is selected. Select the whole hole with the Hole filter.
  • “Case '…': at most one temperature field from a thermal case” / “Time … s is past the end of the transient case (… s).” – one field per case; the snapshot time must lie within the transient duration.
  • “Load case '…' already exists – choose a different name.” – case names must be unique; “Bolt preload” is reserved.
  • “Case '…': reactions are not in equilibrium with the loads … The result is invalid – try a different solver.” – the solver returned an inaccurate solution. In Solve choose another system solver (SPOOLES / PASTIX) or analysis.
